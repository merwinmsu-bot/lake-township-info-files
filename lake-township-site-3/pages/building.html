Best Practices for Starting a Building Project

A guide for residents and property owners — provided with your permit application.

Congratulations on your project! Careful planning protects your investment, your neighbors, and the natural beauty that makes Lake Township special. Following these steps in order will help your project go smoothly and avoid costly delays, stop-work orders, or the expense of moving or removing a non-compliant structure.

Before You Design or Build

  1. Start with the Township — every structure needs a Land Use Permit. ALL structures of any size — including sheds, well houses, decks, and other small buildings — require a Land Use Permit from the Township Zoning Administrator. This is true even when the State Construction Code does not require a building permit for structures under 200 square feet. The Land Use Permit confirms that your project meets zoning requirements, particularly setbacks, before work begins.
  2. Talk to the Zoning Administrator early — before you finalize plans. A short conversation before you design or buy materials can save significant time and money. The Zoning Administrator can tell you your zoning district, the setback, height, and lot-coverage rules that apply, and whether your project needs a Special Use Permit or a variance from the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA). Contact: zoning_admin@laketwp.org or the Township Hall at 231-325-5202.
  3. Know your exact property lines. Setbacks are measured from your legal property lines — not from fences, tree lines, or driveways. You are strongly encouraged to have your property surveyed and staked before you plan the location of any structure. Boundary mistakes are among the most expensive errors a property owner can make.
  4. Review the Zoning Ordinance and check for special conditions on your parcel. The Zoning Ordinance and Master Plan are available on the Township website and at the Township Hall. Also check whether your parcel lies in a floodplain, a critical dune area, a high-risk erosion area along Lake Michigan, or a wetland — these can significantly affect what and where you may build. The Township is currently revising its Zoning Ordinance, so confirm current requirements with the Zoning Administrator before relying on older documents.

Permits You May Need — in the Right Order

  1. Township Land Use Permit (always first). Submit the Land Use Permit Application with a site plan showing property lines, existing structures, the proposed structure, and distances to all lot lines. See the Permit Fee Schedule for current fees. If your project requires a Special Use Permit or ZBA variance, build extra time into your schedule for public notice and hearings.
  2. Benzie County building, electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits. After your Land Use Permit is issued, construction-code permits are handled by Benzie County Building Safety & Construction Code Enforcement — not the Township. County inspections are required at set stages of construction; never cover or conceal work before it has been inspected.
  3. Septic and well permits — Benzie-Leelanau District Health Department. New construction, additions with bedrooms, or changes to water and sewage systems typically require Health Department review and permits for wells and septic systems (bldhd.org).
  4. Shoreline, dune, wetland, and soil-erosion permits. Work along Lake Michigan, Crystal Lake, Platte Lake, Little Platte Lake, or the Platte River may require permits from EGLE, and earth changes near lakes and streams generally require a county soil erosion and sedimentation control permit. Ask before you disturb any ground near the water.
  5. Driveway permit — Benzie County Road Commission. A new driveway, or a change to an existing access onto a public road, requires a permit from the Benzie County Road Commission (benziecrc.org).

During Construction

  1. Call MISS DIG 811 before any digging. It’s free, and it’s the law. Call 811 or visit missdig811.org at least three business days before excavation so underground utilities can be marked.
  2. Hire licensed and insured contractors — and get it in writing. Verify builder licenses through the State of Michigan (michigan.gov/lara), ask for proof of liability and workers’ compensation insurance, and get written contracts and estimates. Confirm who is responsible for pulling permits — and verify the permits were actually issued before work begins.
  3. Keep permits posted and paperwork organized. Post permits as required, keep copies of approved plans on site, and save all inspection records. Complete your final inspections and obtain any required certificate of occupancy before using the new space.
  4. Be a good neighbor. Let adjacent property owners know about your project, keep the site tidy, direct exterior lighting downward, and observe reasonable hours for construction noise. On our lakes and river corridors, take extra care to prevent erosion and runoff.
Questions? We’re happy to help you get it right the first time. Contact the Zoning Administrator at zoning_admin@laketwp.org, call the Township Hall at 231-325-5202 (5153 Scenic Hwy / M-22, Honor, MI 49640), or visit the Planning & Zoning page at laketwp.org for applications, the fee schedule, ordinances, and the Master Plan.

This guide is a summary for convenience only. It does not replace the Lake Township Zoning Ordinance or any county, state, or federal requirement, and requirements may change. When in doubt, ask first.
