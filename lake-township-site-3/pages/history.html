Township History

From lumber and lighthouses to a modern resort community on Benzie County’s Lake Michigan shoreline.

The Township Supervisor is working with the Benzie Area Historical Museum to expand this history with additional photos, documents, and stories. This page will continue to grow.
Lake Township Hall · 5153 Scenic Hwy (M-22), Honor, MI

Lake Township was established in 1873. In the late 1800s, lumbering and commercial fishing were the major economic forces in the township. In 1858, the Point Betsie Lighthouse was established to protect ships passing through the Manitou Passage — as many as 100 sailing or steam vessels could pass Point Betsie in a single day. In 1875, the life-saving station was established at Point Betsie.

Also in 1873, an attempt to build a canal connecting Crystal Lake to Lake Michigan went wrong. The project was not well engineered, and resulted in the “draining of Crystal Lake” — roughly 68 billion gallons of water rushed out, dropping the lake level by 20 feet. This instantly created more than 20 miles of new, sandy shoreline, since developed with summer cottages and year-round homes. The lake level is now controlled by a dam.

In 1883, the towns of Edgewater and Aral were established in Lake Township. Edgewater was located on the northwest shore of Platte Lake, and Aral was located near Otter Creek. A narrow-gauge railroad carried lumber from the Platte Lumber Company to Lake Michigan, where extensive docks loaded it onto ships — some reportedly bound for the rebuilding of Chicago after the great fire of 1871. In 1891, the Otter Creek Lumber Company was established. Lumbering took most of the maple, ash, oak, elm, basswood, hemlock, and beech trees, and by 1910 both towns were gone due to the depletion of the forests.

Chimney Corners Resort was established on the north shore of Crystal Lake in 1935; the original two cottages were built prior to the existence of a road around the lake. Crystal Downs, a world-renowned golf club, was established in 1927. A few farms and orchards were planted beginning in the 1920s — the largest, Crystal Highlands Orchards, grew cherries as its primary crop, alongside apples, blueberries, and Christmas trees. Poor soil conditions made most agricultural operations difficult to sustain, though a few orchards remain in the township today.

The Windjammer Restaurant and river mouth, 1950s — now the site of Lake Township Park

Lake Michigan Road follows the Platte River from M-22 to the Lake Michigan shore. During the 1950s, the Windjammer Restaurant operated at the end of Lake Michigan Road, on the site that is now Lake Township Park. In 1957, the Township purchased the property from the Benzie Development Company for $5,000 — an amount considered to be approximately half of its market value at the time.

The Paddle Princess excursion boat, a beloved Platte River attraction of years past

Lake Township has been a resort community for the past eighty years. The Township encompasses part or all of ten lakes, several of which are now located within the Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore. Beginning in the 1940s, resorts developed along Crystal Lake, Platte Lake, and Little Platte Lake, as Lake Township emerged as a popular vacation destination.

Beginning in the 1950s and continuing into the early 1970s, there was much debate concerning the national importance of the natural beauty of the Lake Michigan shoreline in Benzie and Leelanau Counties. Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore (SBDNL) was officially established in 1970. With some exceptions, most buildings and structures within the park boundaries have been removed and the sites returned to a natural condition.

Many Township residents recognize and appreciate that the creation of the Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore preserved beautiful land in its natural state, remaining accessible to the public for recreation. At the same time, some residents continue to hold lingering concerns or hard feelings about the creation of the SBDNL, particularly descendants of former property owners whose land became part of the National Lakeshore. The National Lakeshore constitutes approximately two-thirds of the land area of Lake Township.

Today, there are a few businesses and other popular non-residential uses, including Crystal Downs Country Club, Point Betsie Lighthouse and Museum, Chimney Corners Resort, and Riverside Canoe Rentals. The remainder of the Township has traditionally consisted primarily of summer homes. In recent years, however, the year-round population has grown as an increasing number of retirees and younger residents have chosen to make Lake Township their permanent home.
