Zoning Board of Appeals · General Information Guide

How the Zoning Board of Appeals works

Board composition, variances, appeals, and hearing procedures for Lake Township, Benzie County, Michigan.

1. Composition & Authority 2. Appeals 3. Dimensional Variances 4. Use Variances 5. Nonconforming Uses 6. Hearings 7. Variance Checklist 8. Statutory Citations

1. Board Composition & Authority

Membership

  • Members are appointed by the Township Board per MCL 125.3601.
  • The board consists of not less than 3 members; members serve 3-year terms after initial staggered appointments.
  • Up to 2 alternate members may be appointed. Alternates may sit in place of an absent member or a member who has abstained due to a conflict of interest.
  • Members may be compensated at a per diem rate set by the Township Board (MCL 125.3601(7)).

Jurisdiction & Authority

  • Hear and decide appeals from any administrative decision made in the enforcement of the Zoning Ordinance (MCL 125.3604(1)).
  • Grant or deny dimensional (non-use) variances based on a showing of practical difficulty (MCL 125.3604(7)).
  • Grant use variances only if expressly authorized in the Township Zoning Ordinance with a 2/3 majority vote required (MCL 125.3604(9)–(10)).
  • Interpret provisions of the Zoning Ordinance or Zoning Map when the meaning is in dispute.
  • Sit in judgment on zoning conflicts and certain other ordinances passed by the Township Board.

Rules of Procedure — Required by Law

The ZBA must adopt written rules of procedure (bylaws) that govern its operations (MCL 125.3603(1)). These bylaws must address at minimum: how and when meetings are called; the time limit for filing an appeal; procedures for conducting hearings, taking testimony, and issuing written decisions; and how conflicts of interest are identified and handled.

Best Practice: Bylaws should also address quorum requirements, voting procedures, how conditions are imposed on approvals, and the process for rehearing requests. The Michigan Municipal League publishes a model ZBA Rules of Procedure that is an excellent starting point.

Conflict of Interest

  • A member with a personal, financial, or familial interest in a matter must disclose the conflict and recuse themselves before any vote.
  • Recused members are replaced by an alternate for that matter only.
  • Undisclosed conflicts of interest can render a ZBA decision void and expose the Township to legal challenge.
Northern Michigan Context: In small townships, conflicts of interest arise frequently. Lake Township should maintain a written conflict-of-interest policy and require members to disclose annually. When in doubt, recuse.

2. Appeals from Administrative Decisions

Any person aggrieved by an administrative decision — such as a zoning administrator’s denial of a permit — or any officer, department, board, or bureau of the Township or State may appeal that decision to the ZBA (MCL 125.3604(1)).

Filing an Appeal

  • An appeal must be filed within the time period prescribed by the ZBA’s bylaws, measured from the date of the administrative decision.
  • The notice of appeal must be filed with: (a) the officer or body whose decision is being appealed, AND (b) the ZBA.
  • The notice must specify the grounds for the appeal.
  • All records, papers, and data that the official used to make the original decision must be transmitted to the ZBA upon filing of the appeal (MCL 125.3604(3)).
Best Practice: Lake Township should establish a clear, written deadline for filing appeals (e.g., 30 days from the date of the administrative decision) in its bylaws. Failure to file within that window typically waives the right to appeal.

Stay of Proceedings

Filing an appeal automatically stays (stops) all proceedings in furtherance of the action being appealed — for example, no enforcement action may continue. The stay is lifted only if the zoning administrator certifies that a stay would cause imminent peril to life or property (MCL 125.3604(4)).

Hearing & Notice Requirements

Once an appeal is filed, the ZBA must (MCL 125.3604(5)): schedule a public hearing within a reasonable time; provide written notice to the applicant; notify registered utilities and all property owners and/or residents within 300 feet of the subject property; and publish notice of the public hearing in a local newspaper between 5 and 15 days before the hearing.

The published notice must include: the nature of the appeal; the property involved; the date, time, and location of the hearing; the date, time, and location where written comments may be submitted; and where the application, Zoning Ordinance, and Zoning Map may be examined.

Best Practice: Consider also posting notice on the Township website and the subject property. In Benzie County, publishing in the Benzie County Record Patriot or Traverse City Record-Eagle satisfies newspaper notice requirements.

ZBA Decision

The ZBA may approve, deny, or approve the appeal with conditions (MCL 125.3604(6)). A simple majority vote is sufficient to decide an appeal. The ZBA must state the grounds for any determination in writing, notify the applicant in writing of the action taken (including any conditions imposed and the reasons for approval or denial), and ensure the written decision is placed in the record.

Judicial Review

A ZBA decision is final at the local level but may be appealed to the Benzie County Circuit Court within 30 days of the decision (MCL 125.3606(1)). The circuit court reviews whether the decision complies with all constitutional and statutory requirements, is supported by competent, material, and substantial evidence on the record, and whether the ZBA exercised reasonable discretion.

Important: The circuit court does not re-hear the case — it reviews the administrative record. A thorough, well-documented record at the ZBA level is essential to defending a decision in court.

3. Dimensional (Non-Use) Variances

A dimensional variance (also called a non-use variance) is a deviation from a physical or dimensional requirement of the Zoning Ordinance — such as a setback, lot coverage limit, height restriction, or lot area — without changing the permitted use of the land. Under MCL 125.3604(7), the ZBA may grant a dimensional variance when the applicant demonstrates practical difficulty.

The Practical Difficulty Standard

Practical difficulty is not defined in the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act, and Michigan case law has not established a single rigid test. However, courts and the Michigan Municipal League have identified the following key factors the ZBA should evaluate:

  • Unique Circumstance — The problem is due to a characteristic of the property itself (e.g., irregular shape, steep topography, wetlands, proximity to shoreline), not a personal hardship of the applicant.
  • Not Self-Created — The difficulty was not caused by the current owner or any previous owner. An existing legally nonconforming structure is not self-created; building too close to the lot line and then requesting a variance is.
  • Not Overly General — The problem is specific to this property, not so widespread in the zoning district that an ordinance amendment would be more appropriate.
  • Strict Compliance Is Unreasonably Burdensome — Applying the ordinance literally would unreasonably prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted legal purpose.
  • Will Not Change the Character of the Area — The variance will not alter the essential character of the surrounding neighborhood or district.
  • Relates Only to the Applicant’s Property — The variance is tied to the physical characteristics of the land, not the desires or circumstances of the current owner.
  • Minimum Necessary — The variance is the least deviation from the ordinance that will allow reasonable use of the property.
  • Substantial Justice — Granting the variance upholds the spirit of the Zoning Ordinance, secures public safety, and does substantial justice (MCL 125.3604(7)).
Northern Michigan Context: Many variance requests in Lake Township involve waterfront properties — irregular lot shapes, narrow lots grandfathered before current setback requirements, or steep bluff terrain. These physical characteristics are strong indicators of genuine practical difficulty. However, the desire for a bigger deck or closer lake view is not a practical difficulty.

What Practical Difficulty Is NOT

  • Financial hardship of the applicant.
  • Personal preference or desire for a particular design.
  • The fact that neighboring properties have similar variances (though this may be relevant to character of the area).
  • The fact that the property could be used more profitably with a variance.

Conditions on Variance Approvals

The ZBA may impose conditions when granting a variance (MCL 125.3604(7)). Conditions must be directly related to and roughly proportional to the variance being granted, tied to one of the ordinance standards used to evaluate the request, and stated clearly in the written decision and recorded with the variance.

Best Practice: Common conditions for waterfront/shoreline variances in Northern Michigan include vegetative screening buffers, limitations on impervious surface, restrictions on future expansion, and stormwater management requirements consistent with Benzie County and EGLE shoreline standards.

Re-Application After Denial

No re-application for a variance on the same property for the same or substantially similar relief may be made within 1 year of a denial, unless the applicant presents new evidence or a change in circumstances that was not available at the time of the original hearing.

4. Use Variances

A use variance allows a property to be used for a purpose not otherwise permitted in its zoning district. Under the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act (MCL 125.3604(9)), use variances are only available to a township if its Zoning Ordinance expressly authorizes them.

Authorization & Voting Requirement

  • Lake Township may only grant use variances if the Zoning Ordinance contains express language authorizing them.
  • A 2/3 supermajority vote of the full ZBA membership is required to approve a use variance (MCL 125.3604(10)).
  • If the Township’s ordinance does not expressly authorize use variances, the ZBA has no authority to grant them.

The Unnecessary Hardship Standard

Use variances require a higher standard of proof — unnecessary hardship — rather than mere practical difficulty. The applicant must typically demonstrate that the property cannot be reasonably used for any permitted use in the zoning district, the hardship is unique to the property and not self-created, and the proposed use will not alter the essential character of the area.

Important: Use variances are disfavored in Michigan law and difficult to obtain. The ZBA should scrutinize use variance requests carefully. Granting a use variance where no unnecessary hardship exists can be challenged and reversed by a circuit court.

5. Nonconforming Structures & Uses

A nonconforming structure or use is one that was legally established before the current Zoning Ordinance took effect but no longer complies with current requirements. These are common throughout Lake Township, particularly along the Crystal Lake shoreline and other older developed areas.

Key Principles

  • A legally nonconforming use or structure has the right to continue — it need not immediately comply with new ordinance requirements.
  • Routine maintenance and interior alterations are generally allowed.
  • Expansion or enlargement of a nonconforming structure may require a variance or special use permit, depending on the ordinance.
  • If a nonconforming use is abandoned (typically defined as cessation for a continuous 12–24 month period), the right to that use is extinguished.
  • If a nonconforming structure is destroyed (typically more than 50% of value), the rebuilt structure must comply with current standards unless the ordinance provides otherwise.

ZBA Role in Nonconforming Situations

The ZBA hears appeals when the zoning administrator makes a determination about nonconforming status, and may grant a dimensional variance to allow limited expansion of a nonconforming structure where practical difficulty is demonstrated. The ZBA does not have authority to simply “grandfather” a use or structure — the legal analysis must follow the ordinance.

Northern Michigan Context: Short-term rental regulations and accessory dwelling unit rules are evolving rapidly across Benzie County townships. Existing rental uses that predate new STR ordinances may or may not constitute protected nonconforming uses — this is an area where careful legal analysis and ZBA consistency are especially important.

6. Conducting Hearings — Best Practices

Before the Hearing

  • Ensure the application is complete and all required materials (site plan, narrative, supporting documents) have been submitted.
  • Confirm proper public notice has been given — newspaper publication, mailed notice to adjacent property owners within 300 feet, and utility notification.
  • Members should review the application packet, staff report, and relevant ordinance sections before the meeting.
  • Members must disclose any conflicts of interest at the start of the hearing.

During the Hearing

  • Open the hearing, state the nature of the application, and confirm the record is being made (audio recording or written minutes).
  • Allow the applicant to present their case first.
  • Provide an opportunity for public comment — both in support and in opposition.
  • Members may ask questions of the applicant and public speakers, but should not advocate or argue.
  • The board should focus questions on the applicable legal standards, not personal opinions about the project.
  • Close the public comment portion before deliberating.

Deliberation & Decision

  • Deliberate openly — the ZBA is subject to the Michigan Open Meetings Act (OMA). No private deliberations.
  • The motion to approve or deny should specifically reference the applicable legal standards and make findings of fact for each.
  • State clearly on the record the reasons for the decision — this is required by law and protects the Township in any appeal.
  • For variances, state whether each practical difficulty factor is met or not met.
  • For conditional approvals, state each condition specifically in the motion.
  • A simple majority vote decides appeals; a 2/3 majority is required for use variances.

Written Decision

After every hearing, the ZBA must issue a written decision that includes the nature of the request, findings of fact addressing each applicable legal standard, the decision (approved, denied, or approved with conditions), any conditions imposed (stated with specificity), and notice that the decision may be appealed to the Benzie County Circuit Court within 30 days.

Best Practice: Keep thorough minutes and maintain a complete record for every hearing. Courts review the administrative record — a well-documented record of findings and reasoning is the Township’s best protection against a successful appeal.

Open Meetings Act (OMA) Compliance

  • All ZBA meetings are subject to the Michigan Open Meetings Act (MCL 15.261 et seq.).
  • Meetings must be open to the public; agendas must be posted at least 18 hours in advance.
  • Minutes must be kept and made available to the public within 8 business days.
  • Executive (closed) sessions are not permitted for ZBA deliberations.
  • Failure to comply with the OMA can invalidate ZBA decisions.

7. Variance Decision Checklist — Dimensional / Non-Use Variance

Use this checklist when evaluating every variance request. The ZBA’s written decision should address each item. All starred items (★) must be satisfied for approval.

Factor (MCL 125.3604(7))Question
★ Unique CircumstanceIs the difficulty due to a physical characteristic of the property — lot shape, topography, shoreline, soil, or similar — rather than personal circumstances of the applicant?
★ Not Self-CreatedWas the condition causing the difficulty created by someone other than the current or a prior owner?
★ Not Overly GeneralIs the problem specific to this property and not so widespread in the district that an ordinance amendment would be more appropriate?
★ Strict Compliance Unreasonably BurdensomeWould strict application of the ordinance prevent any reasonable use of the property for a permitted purpose?
Character of Area PreservedWill the variance preserve the essential character of the surrounding neighborhood?
Relates Only to This PropertyIs the need for a variance tied to this land, not the owner’s personal preferences or financial situation?
Minimum NecessaryIs this the smallest variance that will enable reasonable use? Has the applicant explored alternatives?
★ Substantial JusticeDoes granting the variance uphold the spirit of the ordinance, secure public safety, and do substantial justice?
No Amendment CreatedDoes the variance avoid effectively amending the ordinance for this property class?
Written Decision CompleteDoes the decision state findings of fact for each factor, note conditions, and advise of appeal rights?
Reminder: A variance runs with the land, not the applicant. Once granted, it applies to all future owners of the property. Conditions attached to a variance are also binding on future owners and should be recorded with the Benzie County Register of Deeds.
